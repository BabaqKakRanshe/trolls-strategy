# Steam Next Fest, demo strategy, Steam festivals and itch.io for a small indie strategy game (Troll Strategy)

Research date: 2026-10-05. Tags used below: **[data]** = measured or surveyed numbers; **[rule]** = Valve's documented rule; **[opinion]** = a practitioner's advice with no data behind it. Data older than 2024 is marked **(older)**.

## 1. Next Fest in 2026: rules, timeline, ranking, typical results, what correlates with success, which edition, how far before release

### Takeaway
Next Fest is a one-time-only multiplier, not a discovery engine. In the February 2026 survey, the strongest predictor of what a game gained during the fest was how many wishlists it brought in (Spearman r = 0.825). Games entering with 0–999 wishlists had a median gain of 322. Across all participants, not just survey respondents, the true median is estimated at around 200 or under 500. Because a game gets only one fest, it should be the last edition before a realistic launch, entered with a demo that went live at least a month earlier and with 2,000+ wishlists.

### Cited Findings

**Rules (current Steamworks documentation)**
- [rule] To be eligible, a game must: (a) never have joined a Next Fest before ("titles may only participate in ONE Next Fest"); (b) belong to a Steamworks account in good standing; (c) have a published, public base-game store page; (d) have a publicly playable demo by the time the fest starts; (e) not release before the fest ends; (f) "not [be] a prologue, preview, or short-form version of an existing game already released on Steam." Early Access and free games qualify if they are unreleased. — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [rule] Registration is done on the base game, not the demo. If the demo has a separate store page, you must republish the base game so the backend links the two. A demo can be updated after review without another review and should go live at least 30 minutes before the fest starts. — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [rule] Generic timeline relative to fest start: 5 weeks before, the Developer Preview opens and the demo must pass review if you want Press Preview; 3 weeks before is the final demo submission deadline; Press Preview starts 11 days before; the official trailer goes out 4 days before; the event runs 7 days and Valve publishes wrap-up results at the end. — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [rule] Valve sets no requirement on how close release must be: "There are no requirements regarding when your game releases (so long as it is after Next Fest)." — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [rule] Valve "encourage[s] you to consider leaving your demo up beyond the conclusion" because players "express disappointment when demos disappear immediately." — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [rule] Dates: Next Fest Oct 19–26, 2026; Feb 22–Mar 1, 2027; Jun 14–21, 2027. — [Steamworks: Upcoming events](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events)
- [data, secondary] February 2027 milestones: Valve live Q&A Dec 8, 2026; registration closes Jan 10, 2027, 11:59pm PST; trailers retrieved Jan 18; demo build and store page due for Press Preview Jan 25; all required items due for review Feb 8; Press Preview begins Feb 11; fest Feb 22 (10am PST) to Mar 1. The gap between registration close and fest open is 49 days for Oct 2026, 43 for Feb 2027 and 50 for Jun 2027. — [Steam Page Analyzer, 2026-09-04](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-next-fest-february-2027) (the Valve page for the Feb 2027 edition did not load for me, so these dates come from a secondary source)
- [data, secondary] The June 2027 registration deadline is April 25, 11:59 PM PDT. — [presskit.gg field guide](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration)

**How Valve ranks and features demos**
- [rule] "The first few days of Next Fest are completely randomized in all locations except for explicitly named lists and personalized segments." After that, visibility is personalized "based on an algorithm that's based on user behavior during Next Fest". Games are grouped by 1–2 developer-chosen categories and by Steam tags. The post-event wrap-up features "games with the most played demos". — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [data/opinion] Since October 2024, Valve uses machine-learning visibility and gives every game baseline visibility on days 1–2, so the top games no longer take all the impressions. — [howtomarketagame, Feb 2026 SNF analysis](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- [data, anecdote] One developer got 1,600 wishlists from 900,000 impressions in Feb 2023, versus 350 wishlists from 58,000 impressions in Feb 2026, roughly 15x fewer impressions per entry. — [Abbas Saleem (Llama & Griffin), June 2026 analysis](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/steam-next-fest-june-2026-communities)

**Size of the field (more competition every edition)**
- [data] ~2,244 games in Feb 2025 — [howtomarketagame Feb 2025 benchmarks (archived)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/archive-january-2026-benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/); 2,600+ demos in June 2025 (+13% vs Feb) — [Game World Observer citing GameDiscoverCo](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/06/19/gamediscoverco-named-the-games-with-the-most-wishlists-following-the-june-2025s-steam-next-fest); ~2,900 in Oct 2025 — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-october-2025s-steam-next); ~3,000 in Feb 2026 — [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/); 4,200+ in June 2026, the largest edition so far — [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-next-fest-june-2026/). Saleem counts 4,382 demos in GameDiscoverCo's pool and estimates ~8,682 total entries (+66% YoY). — [Saleem](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/steam-next-fest-june-2026-communities) (an aggregator's estimate, not primary)

**Typical and top wishlist gains**
- [data] Feb 2026 survey (howtomarketagame, 182 responses; 170–174 used in most charts): median 806 wishlists gained, 30th percentile 382, 70th 1.8K, 95th 13.5K, maximum 57.1K. — [howtomarketagame benchmarks (current)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Over time, the survey median fell from 884 (June 2024) to 806 (Feb 2026). The 95th percentile held at about 13,461. — [howtomarketagame Feb 2026 analysis](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Feb 2026 median gain by pre-fest wishlists: 0–999 (n=71): min 41 / p30 179 / **median 322** / p70 485 / max 5,880. 1,000–9,999 (n=60): 74 / 549 / **1,006** / 1,722 / 15,614. 10,000–99,999 (n=36): 515 / 2,584 / **5,215** / 9,633 / 17,342. 100,000+ (n=7): **12,882** median. — [howtomarketagame benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Survey medians are inflated because respondents self-select (they are marketing-active developers). Saleem estimates the true median across all games at about 200 wishlists. — [Saleem](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/steam-next-fest-june-2026-communities). GameDiscoverCo, Oct 2025: the median game added just 18 Steam followers, under 500 wishlists; the top 10% added ~173 followers (~3,000 wishlists); the top 5% ~390 followers (~7,000 wishlists). — [GameDiscoverCo, Oct 2025](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-october-2025s-steam-next)
- [data] Tier labels used in the surveys: Bronze 0–999, Silver 1,000–6,999, Gold 7,000–9,999, Diamond 10,000+. — [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Feb 2025 survey, 208 responses (older, but recent): games with 0–999 pre-fest wishlists had a median gain of 462 (average 705, p30 303, p70 720, max 4,048, about +150%). — [howtomarketagame archived Feb 2025 benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/archive-january-2026-benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Top of the field: in June 2025, Stellar Blade gained 227,200 wishlists during the fest, and #10 (Ratatan) gained 61,900. — [Game World Observer / GameDiscoverCo](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/06/19/gamediscoverco-named-the-games-with-the-most-wishlists-following-the-june-2025s-steam-next-fest). In Oct 2025 five games passed +100k, including the strategy sequel *Heroes of Might and Magic: Olden Era*. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-october-2025s-steam-next). Only about 3–5 games per fest reach 45k–60k. — [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Small-game example: *Freerunners* (Feb 2024, parkour) came in with 420 wishlists and gained 355. It had 154,724 impressions, 2,261 store visits, 1.5% CTR, 497 new demo players and a 6-minute median playtime (demo is 8–10 minutes). It ran no livestream and no press outreach. Russian players were its second-largest audience. — [Game Developer: Freerunners postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/freerunners-steam-next-fest-postmortem)
- [data] **(older, 2020 Valve data)** During the fest, the median game's daily wishlist additions rose 421% compared with the two weeks before. The median game also saw +15% daily additions over the three weeks after. Fest wishlists convert to sales at about 0.75x the rate of pre-fest wishlists. — [GameDiscoverCo, 2021](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-demo-showcases-officially-good)

**What correlates with good results**
- [data] Feb 2026 Spearman correlations with wishlists gained during the fest: total pre-fest wishlists r = 0.825; wishlists gained in the 2 weeks before r = 0.819; demo conversion rate r = 0.457; how early the demo launched r = −0.205 (weak). — [howtomarketagame benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Demo conversion rate (players who played and wishlisted ÷ demo players), Feb 2026: p30 12%, median 16.33%, p70 20%. "The magic number for Demo conversion rate seems to be about the 20% range." — [howtomarketagame benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Most wishlists come from people who never play the demo. In Feb 2025, players who wishlisted without playing made up 68% (Diamond) to 88% (Bronze) of wishlists, and played-and-wishlisted conversion by tier was Diamond 32%, Gold 14.7%, Silver 19.5%, Bronze 17.8%. So the capsule, trailer and page do most of the work. — [howtomarketagame Feb 2025 archive](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/archive-january-2026-benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Demos that launched well before the fest earned about 2.5x the wishlists of late launches. Of the 10 top games (95th percentile) in Feb 2026, 5 had demos out months early, 4 about a month before, and only 1 shadow-dropped a few days before. — [howtomarketagame Feb 2026 analysis](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Hosting the demo on a separate page versus the main page showed no significant difference for games under 10k pre-fest wishlists (Feb 2026). In Feb 2025, separate pages had a median of 1,040.5 and the main page 1,300.5. — [howtomarketagame current](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/), [Feb 2025 archive](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/archive-january-2026-benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [data] Feb 2025 Diamond-tier genres included Strategy (2), Management (2) and Autobattler (1), so these genres can perform. — [howtomarketagame Feb 2025 archive](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/archive-january-2026-benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [opinion] Enter with at least 2,000 wishlists. Get the demo out well ahead and iterate on it. Avoid shadow drops ("It is much safer to take a lot of shots on goal"). Do smaller festivals before Next Fest. — [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [opinion] Press Preview: "Press coverage that includes actual gameplay impressions is significantly more valuable than coverage based solely on your store page." — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration)
- [rule] Livestreaming is optional but encouraged, and you stream directly to the store page. — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)

**February vs June vs October; distance from release**
- [opinion] Choose "the Next Fest closest to (but before) their planned launch date": a spring 2027 launch means the Feb 2027 fest, summer or autumn 2027 means June 2027. The guide characterizes February as post-holiday with less AAA competition, June as overlapping Summer Game Fest with more media noise and more browsing players, and October as the lead-in to holiday sales, where horror overperforms. "Next Fest is a multiplier, not a generator." — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration)
- [opinion] "February has historically been the largest fest by entry count." The same source says "wishlists collected six months before release have more time to go cold" but gives no numbers. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-next-fest-february-2027)
- [opinion] Launch the game "a few weeks later" after the fest, not immediately. — [howtomarketagame Feb 2025 archive](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/archive-january-2026-benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/). Against the "wishlists go cold" view, Zukowski's case study of Cosmoteer argues that "wishlists don't get stale" over a 4.5-year campaign. — [howtomarketagame (2023, older)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/02/06/how-cosmoteers-4-5-year-marketing-campaign-shows-that-wishlists-dont-get-stale/)

### Inferences
- Troll Strategy has no audience, so a realistic baseline is the 0–999 bucket: a median of about 300 wishlists from the fest (survey), and possibly about 200 or fewer (true median). Breaking out would take 2,000+ pre-fest wishlists and a demo-to-wishlist conversion of 20% or better.
- The October 2026 edition (Oct 19–26) is out of reach because its registration closed about 49 days before.
- **February 2027** requires: a public Coming Soon page and Next Fest registration by Jan 10, 2027; a reviewed demo by Jan 25 to be in Press Preview; everything by Feb 8; and release after Mar 1, 2027. Because a demo should be public at least about a month before the fest, it would need to be live by about mid-January 2027. That is roughly 3 months from today, with missions 2–7 and saves not yet built, and Feb 2027 only makes sense if the game launches in spring 2027.
- **June 2027** gives roughly 6 more months to make the demo, publish the store page, gather wishlists through third-party festivals and a public demo, and iterate. That matches all the data-backed levers (pre-fest wishlists, an early demo, a better conversion rate), provided launch is about July–Q4 2027.
- Since the fest is one-time-only, choosing the edition by launch date matters more than which month is "best"; I found no data showing one edition is better than another.

### Gaps
- I found no recent survey that isolates the effect of livestreams, Press Preview or capsule quality on fest results. The surveys report only pre-fest wishlists, velocity, demo timing and conversion. Practitioner claims on these points are opinion.
- No numeric comparison of Feb vs June vs Oct editions (traffic, conversion) beyond entry counts.
- Valve's page for the Feb 2027 edition did not load, so those dates come from a secondary source (Steam Page Analyzer) and should be checked in Steamworks.
- No Next Fest postmortem with numbers for a colony/automation/logistics builder from 2024–2026 was found.

## 2. Demo best practices: length, content slice, timing, median playtime, wishlist prompts, separate page and reviews, keeping it up, updating, cannibalization

### Takeaway
Ship the demo publicly months before the fest. Give it its own page so it collects reviews. Keep it up permanently and treat demo-to-wishlist conversion (aim for about 20%) and median playtime as the health metrics. The 30–40-minute plan fits the playtime data: high-performing demos show 38–65-minute median playtimes, but that data is from 2022. Fears that a demo cannibalizes sales rest on 2013 console data and are not supported for Steam indies.

### Cited Findings
- [rule] A demo can get its own optional store page, with a description, feature list, languages and **user reviews**, linking back to the full game. Demos "do not have their own community hub." Demos "can appear anywhere in Steam that a free game could appear" (New & Trending, tag and genre pages). — [Steamworks: Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos)
- [rule] On first making the demo playable, you can trigger a one-time email and mobile notification to everyone who wishlisted the full game. It is available for 2 weeks after demo launch. — [Steamworks: Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos)
- [rule] Valve's guidance: quality must "measure up to the real experience". On timing, normally wait for launch, but release early "if you feel like customers need to touch the game to really understand it". On length, give "enough content to get them excited, without giving away so much that they feel like they've experienced everything". A deactivated demo stays in players' libraries but cannot be launched. — [Steamworks: Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos)
- [data] July 26, 2024 Valve update: separate demo pages, demo reviews, and demos listed like free games in New & Trending and on tag pages (with free-product thresholds adjusted). Players can add a demo to their library without installing it. Valve said this responds to developers shipping "prologue"/"chapter one" free apps to get store visibility. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/demos-update-free-games)
- [data] **(older, 2022, about 130 games)** Demo median playtime across surveyed demos: median 14 min, average 19.75 min, p30 10 min, p70 22 min. Median playtime by tier: Bronze 7 min, Silver 18 min, Gold 38 min, Diamond 65 min. At the Oct 2022 fest the median was 18 min. Soulstone Survivors (about 3 h median) and Manor Lords (1 h 20 min) topped the fest. Capital Command still earned 22,862 wishlists with a 10-minute median. — [Steam Page Analyzer summarizing Zukowski, 2026-09-23](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-long-should-a-steam-demo-be); see also [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=2409)
- [data] **(older)** Cosmoteer's demo had a 113-minute median playtime, and the game reached 144,000 wishlists at launch. Its earlier free itch.io demo regularly had 1,000+ concurrent players. — [howtomarketagame (2023)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/02/06/how-cosmoteers-4-5-year-marketing-campaign-shows-that-wishlists-dont-get-stale/)
- [opinion] Recommended designed length is "30 to 90 minutes", with factory/colony sims and strategy/4X at 60–90 minutes and the first hook in under 10 minutes. This is community consensus, not cited data. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-long-should-a-steam-demo-be)
- [opinion] "If fewer than 40% of players finish your demo, it's probably too long." The source suggests 20–40 minutes. — [Steam Page Analyzer, 2026-03-03](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-demo-best-practices)
- [data] Healthy demo-to-wishlist conversion is a median of 16.33% with a "magic number" around 20% (Feb 2026 survey). — [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- [opinion, unverified number] An end-of-demo wishlist button is described as the highest-converting placement (end screen, then pause menu, then main menu), implemented with `SteamFriends.ActivateGameOverlayToStore()`. The claim that it adds "5–10 percentage points" of conversion comes with no source. The same article also wrongly says demos have "no separate page", which contradicts Valve's documentation, so treat this source with caution. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-demo-best-practices)
- [data] Release the demo before the fest. Demos out 1+ month before outperformed by about 2.5x, though r = −0.205 shows much overlap. — [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- [data/opinion] **Keep it up.** Zukowski argues demos work through streamers discovering them, sometimes months later. Dome Keeper earned about 40,000 wishlists in one month through streamers and its demo. Mob Factory (tower defense/automation) got a spike 3 days after its demo, when streamer Wanderbots covered it. He dismisses taking the demo down for FOMO: people don't play demos "because they don't know the game exists." — [howtomarketagame (2023, older)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/03/07/why-demos-dont-hurt-your-visibility/). Valve also encourages keeping demos up after the fest. — [Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [opinion] A contrary view: pull the demo 2–4 weeks before launch if it has been out 3+ months. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-demo-best-practices)
- [data/opinion] **Cannibalization.** The "demo kills sales" evidence comes from a 2013 DICE talk on console data. Zukowski argues it does not apply to Steam indies, where the demo's value is streamer exposure plus wishlists. — [howtomarketagame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/03/07/why-demos-dont-hurt-your-visibility/). The main risk named is short narrative games, where a demo plus Steam's 2-hour refund window can make buying optional (opinion). — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-long-should-a-steam-demo-be)
- [rule/opinion] **Updating.** Valve allows demo updates without re-review. — [Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest). Practitioners advise shipping early, checking median time played weekly, adding checkpoint analytics (tutorial, first battle, demo end), fixing the opening if the median is under 10 minutes, and not changing content mid-fest. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-long-should-a-steam-demo-be)
- [opinion] Go into the fest with a big demo update and a polished demo. — [presskit.gg / Steam Page Analyzer search summary](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration)

### Inferences
- A 30–40-minute demo is in the right range for a management/strategy game. The key data-backed metric is that players stay long (median 20+ minutes) and convert at about 20%. The demo slice should reach the hook early: the first full mine→smelter→forge→market chain running, plus one auto-battle with squad gear and placement within the first ~10 minutes.
- The game already has a drop-off funnel in telemetry, which covers the checkpoint analytics that practitioners recommend. Steamworks only provides median playtime and wishlist conversion.
- The demo should not need saves: a 30–40-minute session can run without them, but a crash or quit then resets progress. It is worth checking the funnel for abandonment around minutes 20–40.

### Gaps
- No primary dataset quantifies the effect of end-of-demo wishlist prompts. The 5–10 pp figure is unsourced.
- No 2024–2026 survey of demo median playtime. The tier data is from 2022.
- No hard data on demo cannibalization for strategy or management games on Steam.

## 3. Steam Playtest vs itch.io for closed testing

### Takeaway
Steam Playtest is a no-cost, key-free closed or open beta attached to your Coming Soon page. It gives no visibility and does not affect wishlists or reviews. It suits controlled testing once the store page exists. itch.io (restricted or unlisted pages) is the option before a store page exists.

### Cited Findings
- [rule] Playtest is a separate child appID. Players request access through a button on the main game's store page; there is no separate store page. Signup is either limited (you grant access in batches, the default) or open (automatic). You can request up to about 50,000 keys versus 2,500 for the main game. — [Steamworks: Playtest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)
- [rule] "A customer's wishlist for your game won't be impacted when they join or leave your playtest". Playtest-only players "cannot review your actual game". Charging is not allowed. Feedback collection "is up to you" (Discord, forums, your own tools). A playtest can run indefinitely or be set to "Not Playable". It can be reset, removing all participants. An experimental friend-invite option allows 1–3 invites per player. — [Steamworks: Playtest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)
- [rule] Valve suggests running playtests "before your game releases", after a Coming Soon page exists. — [Steamworks: Playtest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)
- [opinion] A playtest "buys you nothing in marketing terms". Only the demo generates wishlists, enters Next Fest and appears in store lists. Playtests are found only by people already on your store page. — [Steam Page Analyzer: Playtest vs Demo](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-playtest-vs-demo)

### Inferences
- Once the Coming Soon page is public, a limited-signup Steam Playtest is the better closed-test channel than itch for Troll Strategy. It needs no key handling, uses the same SteamPipe build pipeline as the demo, leaves no public reviews of a rough build, and sends signups to the store page, where they can also wishlist. Existing telemetry gives the feedback loop that Playtest lacks.
- itch remains useful before the store page goes live, or for quick feedback from a small, invited group.

### Gaps
- I found no data on playtest signups converting to wishlists or sales.

## 4. Themed Steam fests and third-party festivals: eligibility, fit for a colony/logistics builder with auto-battles, how to find and apply, results

### Takeaway
Valve's themed fests accept Coming Soon games with demos. Valve marks eligibility automatically, mainly from tags and descriptions, about 2 months ahead, and games opt in. Unlike Next Fest they are not one-time. The H1 2027 list has no city-builder, colony or automation fest, and Troll Strategy is unlikely to qualify for RTS Fest or Shop Keeper Fest on genre. Third-party festivals (about 470 tracked) favour "Cozy, Crafty, Buildy, Management" games, which is the best fit, and should be used repeatedly before Next Fest to build pre-fest wishlists.

### Cited Findings

## 5. itch.io as a channel; separate short itch build vs Steam demo; prologue app vs demo

### Takeaway
For a Steam-bound premium game, itch.io is a testing and feedback channel, not a reliable source of wishlists. The one 2025 data point I found shows about 3% of itch browser plays turning into Steam wishlists, against about 16–20% conversion for Steam demos. Browser builds get far more plays than downloads (anecdotal). The separate prologue-app trick has been largely replaced by Valve's July 2024 demo upgrades, and a prologue of a released game cannot enter Next Fest.

### Cited Findings
- [data, single case] r/IndieDev (about Nov 2025), a complex strategic incremental game in a **browser** build: about 6,500 itch views, about 5,300 browser plays (81% play rate), about 150 Steam wishlists from itch players (**~3%**), plus about 100 from organic Steam traffic. The Steam demo had about 1,600 installs and about 200 launches, with a median of 23 minutes in a demo of about 40 minutes. Commenters called 3% "okay, not that great". — [Reddit snapshot: "How many Steam Wishlists can 5,000 itch.io plays convert into?"](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1r359kv/snapshots/2026-02-13T08%3A10%3A21.68974Z)
- [data, anecdote; low-quality aggregator] Browser games reportedly get about 3x the engagement of download-only games, and one developer reported 25% of views converting to web plays, "8 times better" than downloads. — [Cinevva guide (aggregator, unverified)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/get-more-plays-itch-io)
- [data, older] Cosmoteer's free itch.io demo is an outlier success (1,000+ concurrent players) after years of building an audience. — [howtomarketagame (2023)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/02/06/how-cosmoteers-4-5-year-marketing-campaign-shows-that-wishlists-dont-get-stale/)
- [opinion] "Wishlists are everything on Steam", while itch collections bring no comparable benefit. — [kymira bearblog (via search summary)](https://kymira.bearblog.dev/a-beginners-journey-through-indie-marketing/)
- [rule] Next Fest excludes any "prologue, preview, or short-form version of an existing game already released on Steam." — [Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- [data, older 2023] The prologue trick: a separate free app ($100 fee), a 30–45-minute core experience, a CTA to the main game. Soulstone Survivors got about 130,000 wishlists from its prologue. Risks: player confusion, three builds to maintain, wasted effort if the prologue never reaches Popular Upcoming. Zukowski later added that it "is probably going away soon due to Steam's changes to the Demo algorithms." — [howtomarketagame (2023, updated 2024)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/04/03/how-to-use-the-prologue-trick-to-potentially-earn-thousands-of-wishlists/)
- [data] Valve's July 2024 demo update (separate pages, reviews, free-game list visibility) explicitly responded to the prologue trend. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/demos-update-free-games)

### Inferences
- A separate 10-minute itch build adds a second build to maintain (content cut, ending CTA, QA, uploads through butler) for a channel that, in the only data point found, turns about 3% of players into wishlists. It would be worth it only as a browser (WebGL) build, which gets far more plays, and only if the 3D Unity game with UI Toolkit runs acceptably in WebGL; that is unverified.
- A cheaper alternative: put the same Steam demo build on itch as a download, with a Steam widget or link, and use itch mainly for jams, devlogs and feedback from Russian-speaking communities. Keep the 30–40-minute Steam demo, on its own page, as the one public demo.
- The game already has a drop-off funnel, so it can measure how many itch players reach the end-of-build Steam link before deciding whether a dedicated itch cut is worth it.
- Do not create a separate "Prologue" app. A demo with its own page gives the same reviews and visibility, costs nothing extra, and keeps Next Fest eligibility simple.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 dataset of typical itch.io traffic for a downloadable 3D strategy game.
- No published conversion data for itch download builds to Steam wishlists. The only case found is a browser build.
- Unity WebGL performance for this game (3D, UI Toolkit, simulation) is untested. That is a technical question outside this research.
